# Explanation stance (authoring)

The register an authored **explanation** page answers to. Load it when the unit's
declared direction is explanation; the reference path never loads it. It sits
above `references/writing-standards.md` the same way the reference stance does —
writing-standards.md governs what the text is and how it treats its own history
for every direction; this file governs the explanation register on top of it. It
does NOT replace the per-format markup standards (MyST vs rST, held in the SKILL.md
step 3); those still apply. What it does replace is the reference direction's
neutral register: an explanation page is not written as a look-it-up inventory.

## The understanding-oriented register — the governing stance

Explanation is **understanding-oriented**.
It explains why things are the way they are.
Where reference states what is and stops, explanation gives the reader the
**rationale**, the **context** a decision was made in, the **trade-offs** weighed,
and the **connections** between this concept and the ones around it. The reader
arrives already able to look up the facts; they leave understanding why the facts
are shaped the way they are.

Write to that end. A good explanation page:

- **Names the rationale** behind a design, a default, or a constraint — the reason
  the thing is the way it is, not merely that it is that way.
- **Supplies the context** the decision lived in — the problem it answered, the
  alternative it was chosen over.
- **Weighs the trade-offs** honestly — what the choice costs as well as what it
  buys; explanation that only praises is marketing, not understanding.
- **Draws the connections** — how this concept relates to its neighbours, why it
  sits where it sits in the larger design.

This is the opposite of the reference direction's austere "look it up" voice. Do
not import the reference stance contract's "write as the product's reference"
register — it is reference-specific and belongs only to that direction. An
explanation page reads as a knowledgeable colleague explaining why, grounded in
what the sources and the recorded intent actually establish.

## Recorded intent is the primary source

The unit's declared `sources` still ground every factual claim. But for an
explanation unit the **recorded intent steering is the primary source** for the
*why* — it is where the rationale, the context, and the weighed trade-offs were
crystallized during crayon-plan's intent walk. Read the whole steering stream
before authoring, alongside the declared sources; it carries the understanding the
page exists to convey.

**Curated, never pasted wholesale.** Steering text is raw intake — the TA's own
words captured during the walk. It *informs* the prose; it is never dropped into
the page verbatim. Curate it: distil the settled understanding into publishable
explanation, the same curated-excerpts discipline the reference direction applies
to its sources, now applied to intent. Internal steering phrasing, half-formed
asides, and TA shorthand stay out of the published page.

## Never invent rationale

The register invites a specific failure the reference direction does not: inventing
a plausible-sounding *why* the sources and steering never established. Do not.
Every rationale, trade-off, and causal claim ("X was chosen because…", "this
avoids…") must be grounded in the recorded intent steering or the declared
sources — pass the three-check grounding rule in `references/writing-standards.md`
(locate, confirm, corroborate) before it enters prose. A rationale you cannot
ground goes into the report's gaps, never into the page as confident
explanation. A fabricated *why* is worse than a missing one: it reads as authority
and misleads.

## What generalizes from writing-standards.md

`references/writing-standards.md` is register-neutral and applies here in full —
do not restate it, point at it: end-state writing (write the page to its correct
end-state, delete what a change obsoletes, diff the rewrite), the three-check
grounding rule and its inverse "never invent a constraint" (extended above to
"never invent rationale"), the bidirectional staleness scan, the writer
micro-rules (single-antecedent pronouns, no self-reference, say-once-link-
elsewhere), and the after-write mechanics. This file adds only the explanation
register on top of that shared base; the base is byte-frozen.
